# Research vision: hydrogen retention and loss in water-bearing geological formations

**Researcher:** Kudakwashe Marara  
**Current stage:** independent exploratory research; postgraduate direction under development  
**Primary scientific theme:** hydrogen retention and loss in water-bearing geological formations

## 1. Central question

How do brine chemistry, pore confinement and mineral–fluid interactions influence hydrogen dissolution, retention and loss in geological formations considered for underground hydrogen storage?

This question is deliberately narrower than a general programme covering every aspect of underground hydrogen storage. It centres on hydrogen behaviour in water-bearing geological media. Experimental fluid-property evidence, pore-scale mechanisms and geological screening are supporting components of this one problem.

## 2. Motivation from prior research

Zhang and co-authors' 2023 *Energy & Fuels* study examines H₂ dissolution in water-saturated kaolinite nanopores under nanoconfinement. Their 2023 *ACS Omega* study examines H₂/CH₄ mixture storage and adsorption in kaolinite nanopores. These studies motivate investigation of pore size, water saturation, brine chemistry and mineral surface structure.

The relevant research opportunity is not to claim that an interfacial-tension dataset validates nanopore solubility. IFT, solubility and adsorption are distinct properties. Rather, the current IFT source-audit work provides experience in establishing reliable experimental evidence, and a separate solubility evidence programme could be developed if suitable primary measurements can be obtained.

## 3. Research objectives

1. Establish traceable experimental evidence for relevant H₂–brine fluid properties, beginning with the ongoing IFT source-verification work and assessing the feasibility of a separate H₂-solubility dataset.
2. Compare how reported brine chemistry, temperature, pressure, water saturation, pore size and mineralogy affect hydrogen dissolution or adsorption across suitable experimental and simulation studies.
3. Identify where bulk-fluid measurements can and cannot be used to interpret confined pore-scale behaviour.
4. Develop the skills needed to evaluate pore-scale mechanisms, including molecular simulation if suitable supervision, training and computational resources are available.
5. Explore how validated evidence could eventually inform geological screening, while avoiding unsupported field-scale safety or site-ranking claims.

## 4. Current foundation and limitations

The researcher has a BSc (Hons) in Petroleum Chemistry and Technology (Upper Second Class, 2.1) from the University of Zimbabwe and laboratory experience at the National Oil Infrastructure Company. The NOIC work included a 10-week immersion investigation of protective paint/epoxy coating systems in ethanol–petrol blend environments, immersion testing, degradation calculations, procedures and reporting under relevant ASTM/ISO requirements.

An independent H₂–brine IFT source-verification project has produced a 158-observation stage-one candidate tranche from Chow et al. (2018), Hosseini et al. (2022) and Mirchi et al. (2022). It is not yet a released benchmark: global overlap review and full release validation remain outstanding. The Jiao et al. (2026) 450-row secondary compilation has been audited group by group; numerical matches do not automatically establish eligibility, and unresolved source and metadata issues are documented.

A preliminary Python geospatial site-screening framework also exists. It uses coarse geological inputs and exploratory weighted-overlay sensitivity analysis. It does not currently model hydrogen loss, mineral sensitivity, microbial effects or caprock integrity, and its outputs are not validated safety assessments.

The researcher has not yet conducted molecular-dynamics simulations. This is a training need, not a claimed skill. The legacy PINN implementation has been audited and is not described as a validated Peng–Robinson-constrained PINN.

## 5. Proposed methodological pathway

### Work package A: evidence and data quality

- Identify primary experimental sources and supplementary datasets.
- Extract original values with page/table/figure coordinates, original units and conditions.
- Preserve electrolyte identity, gas composition, concentration basis, experimental method and reported uncertainty.
- Record data lineage, duplicates, conversions and unresolved ambiguities.
- Keep IFT, solubility, adsorption and simulation outputs as distinct evidence types.

### Work package B: pore-scale mechanisms

- Conduct a focused literature synthesis of H₂ dissolution and adsorption in water-bearing mineral nanopores.
- Identify comparable mineral surfaces, pore geometries, water saturation states, brine compositions, temperatures and pressures.
- Learn the molecular simulation methods and software selected by the host research group.
- Where feasible, reproduce a small, published benchmark case before proposing new simulations.
- Compare simulation outputs only with experiments that measure the same physical property under sufficiently comparable conditions.
- Quantify uncertainty and document model assumptions and limits.

The specific simulation method, force field, software and validation data must be selected with the supervisor. They are not predetermined here.

### Work package C: geological implications

- Review how pore-scale dissolution and adsorption evidence relates to hydrogen retention and loss.
- Identify which mineralogical and geochemical indicators are available at regional scale and their resolution limitations.
- Treat the existing GIS framework as exploratory infrastructure only.
- Do not convert pore-scale results directly into field-scale safety scores without an appropriate upscaling model and validation.

## 6. Expected outputs

Potential outputs, contingent on results and supervisor guidance:
- A reproducible source-verified H₂–brine fluid-property evidence dataset and audit report.
- A critical review or comparative study of hydrogen dissolution/adsorption in water-bearing nanopores.
- A validated reproduction of a selected published pore-scale simulation case, if training and resources permit.
- A master's thesis linking experimental evidence to pore-scale hydrogen retention mechanisms.
- One or more manuscripts if the work produces sufficiently novel, validated results.

No publication or performance outcome is guaranteed.

## 7. Training needs

The researcher can contribute petroleum chemistry knowledge, laboratory testing experience, Python, Excel, Git/GitHub, literature verification and reproducible data handling. Training is needed in molecular simulation, relevant thermodynamic and transport theory, pore-scale modelling and appropriate validation. These methods should be learned through the host group's established protocols and supervision.

## 8. Scope discipline

The central topic is hydrogen retention and loss in water-bearing geological formations. IFT benchmarking supports experimental evidence quality; H₂-solubility evidence is a proposed separate dataset; site screening is a downstream application. These are not three independent thesis topics. The master's project should be narrowed with the supervisor to a tractable question based on the group's expertise, available data, facilities and time.
