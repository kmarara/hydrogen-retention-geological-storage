## Paper 1: nanoconfined hydrogen dissolution

Zhang et al. (2023), “Molecular Simulation of H₂ Loss by Dissolution in Caprock Water-Saturated Nanopores under the Nanoconfinement Effect for Underground Hydrogen Storage,” *Energy & Fuels* 37(23). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.3c03384

The abstract and research notes report that nanoconfinement can increase hydrogen dissolution relative to bulk solubility, that smaller water-saturated pores are important to the reported loss, and that higher brine salinity reduces hydrogen loss in the studied system.

**Relevance:** motivates questions about how pore size, water saturation and brine chemistry affect hydrogen retention. Do not claim that IFT data validate the paper's solubility results. IFT and solubility are different properties.

## Paper 2: H₂/CH₄ adsorption and storage in kaolinite

Zhang et al. (2023), “Molecular Simulation of H₂/CH₄ Mixture Storage and Adsorption in Kaolinite Nanopores for Underground Hydrogen Storage,” *ACS Omega* 8(48). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.3c06475

The research notes identify water effects and differences between gibbsite and siloxane surfaces as relevant findings. Re-open the full paper before quoting precise numerical findings.

## Paper 3: UHS challenges and outlook

Luo, Jia & Zhang (2023), “Technical challenges and outlook of underground hydrogen storage in depleted oil and gas reservoirs,” *Oil & Gas Storage and Transportation* 42(9).

The research notes report discussion of hydrogen-sensitive minerals, ions and microorganisms, as well as facility-material compatibility concerns. Verify the original text and bibliographic details before quoting it in a formal proposal or email.
